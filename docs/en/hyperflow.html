HyperFlow H3 Flash (a few-step model)

HyperFlow H3 Flash is a derivative of MiniMax H3 by Video Rebirth: the video part of the model (the DiT) is distilled and compressed so that it finishes in 4, 6 or 8 steps. H3cApp now supports its GGUF files experimentally. With the same prompts and seeds you can compare it with the Turbo LoRA, DMAD and regular 20-step runs.

Compare the results

All video and sound on this page is AI-generated (in H3cApp, with MiniMax H3 and its derivative HyperFlow H3 Flash). Three prompts, each generated once with six methods (672x384, 5 s, seed 42). Choose a clip and a method for each side, then use “Still” (frame 60) for detail and “Video” for motion. The page opens with the Turbo LoRA (4 steps) on the left and HyperFlow (6 steps) on the right.

Different methods give different scenes even with the same seed. What to compare is not the same detail in the same place, but how clean a picture each method produces. At “Quarter speed” it is easier to see whether fur or the background wobbles.

Prompts used
  • Cat: A small orange cat plays with a ball of yarn on a wooden floor in soft afternoon light. Static camera, gentle movement. Soft room ambience, no speech, no music.
  • Guitar: A street musician plays an acoustic guitar in a quiet stone plaza at dusk, warm lights in the windows behind him. Slow push-in camera. Gentle guitar melody, distant footsteps, no speech.
  • Talking: A young woman with short black hair, wearing a navy blazer, stands in a bright university hallway and speaks directly to the camera with a friendly smile, saying: "Welcome to our campus. Let me show you around." Medium close-up, static camera. Clear voice, quiet indoor ambience, no music.

Every clip was AI-generated from text alone in H3cApp (2026-11-09, M5 with 24 GB). The videos were re-compressed for this page, so fine grain is lower than in the actual output.

  • Quality: HyperFlow at 6 and 8 steps is about on par with Turbo at 4 steps: about one point apart in MUSIQ, a slightly better prompt match and about half the flicker.
  • Time: on the M5, HyperFlow at 4 steps takes about as long as Turbo, about 1.2 times as long at 6 steps and about 1.45 times at 8. It is not faster on the M5.
  • Memory: on the M5 (24 GB) it runs resident (int8) and peaked at 17-18 GiB. With SSD streaming it peaked at 8.5 GiB and reads 7.8 GiB per step, against 10.1 GiB for the regular GGUF (Q4_K).

The differences between prompts are larger than those between methods; the scores are a rough guide from three clips each. Averages over six clips each, adding seed 314 for Turbo, DMAD and HyperFlow at 6 steps, show the same pattern.

Watch with sound

Like the regular MiniMax H3, HyperFlow makes the video and its sound together. These are AI-generated HyperFlow 6-step results as they came out, with sound (press play; nothing plays by itself).

What the model is

  • A video part rebuilt for few steps: MiniMax H3's video part (the DiT), distilled to finish in few steps. It uses no CFG (no comparison with a negative prompt) and generates with the model's own time schedules for 4, 6 and 8 steps.
  • Smaller weights: FL2VA (from text, or from first and last frames) has 13.7B parameters (47 blocks, 18 of them with low-rank factored linears) and Ref2VA 13.9B (49 blocks). At GGUF Q4_K_M the FL2VA DiT is 9.05 GB.
  • The other parts are the same: the text encoder and the video and audio VAEs are MiniMax H3's own.
  • How it differs from the Turbo LoRA: the Turbo LoRA is an add-on for the regular model; HyperFlow replaces the model itself. So the features made for the regular model, such as LoRAs, do not work with HyperFlow (what it cannot do).

 Goals and what to choose. Which one suits you depends on the scene.

With HyperFlow the same seed often keeps a similar composition across step counts (almost the same in the "Talking" clip above). You can look for a good take at 4 steps and remake the seed you like at 8. Sometimes, as with the guitar, 4 steps gives a different scene.

How to use it

Available from v0.8.0 (experimental).

  1. Have a regular MiniMax H3 model ready. HyperFlow's text encoder and VAEs are cloned from an imported MiniMax H3 model (Using GGUF).
  2. Import the HyperFlow GGUF. In the download wizard, set the DiT (FL2VA) source to VideoRebirthAI/hyperflow-h3-flash and pick a file from fl2va_gguf/ (Q4_K_M is the tested one), or import a file you have with “Import GGUF…”. H3cApp recognizes HyperFlow from the file's contents and shows it in the model details (ⓘ).
  3. Switch to the HyperFlow model and generate. Choose 4, 6 or 8 steps (6 by default). Settings that do not apply, such as the speed presets and LoRAs, are hidden in the advanced settings. Switching back to a regular model restores your settings.

The app's API (POST /api/generate) works the same way. With a HyperFlow model, steps accepts only 4, 6 and 8, and settings that do not apply get a 400 with the reason.

What it cannot do

  • Support is experimental. The default model stays the regular MiniMax H3.
  • Not available: step counts other than 4, 6 and 8, speed presets, denoiser reuse, the layer count, add-on models (LoRA), re-noise and Open-H3 regeneration. No int8 cache is built.
  • Compute mode: on an M5 with at least 24 GB it runs resident (int8) with “int8 cache” selected (peak 17-18 GiB). Other Macs use SSD streaming.
  • What has been checked: generation from text with the FL2VA Q4_K_M, and generation from a reference image, a reference video and reference audio with the Ref2VA Q4_K_M (one clip per kind of reference, both resident and with SSD streaming). M1-M4 behaviour was checked by emulating them on the M5 with its newer GPU features turned off (the three Ref2VA clips). Generation from first and last frames, and running on actual M1-M4 Macs, have not been checked yet.
  • Records: the video's record (.json) has modelFamily: "hyperflow" and its XMP AISystemUsed says "HyperFlow H3 Flash (MiniMax H3 derivative)".

How we compared

  • All six methods were generated through H3cApp's API with the same prompts, the same seed (42) and the same size (672x384, 5 s). The Turbo LoRA was v1.2; the DMAD LoRA used its per-LoRA automatic sampler (re-noise); the regular model was the GGUF (Q4_K) running with the int8 cache.
  • The quality estimate (MUSIQ) and the prompt match (CLIPScore) are means over every 8th frame. Flicker was measured over all frames.
  • Besides the scores, four frames of each clip (first, 40, 80, last) were checked by eye at full size. The sound was not compared by ear.
  • The regular model's runs overlapped with a large file copy, so their times may be a few seconds long.
  • Block by block against the reference implementation (ComfyUI on CUDA), the engine's final output differs by 3-8 % resident (int8) and 2-5 % with SSD streaming (BF16).
